I am a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering and Applied Mechanics at the University of Pennsylvania and an NSF Graduate Research Fellow. I work in the ScalAR Lab with Dr. Ani Hsieh, and alongside the PhD, I am partway through a master’s in Robotics. I am currently on a medical leave of absence from Penn.

My research is on learned dynamics and physics-informed machine learning for robots. At Penn, I built a knowledge-based neural ODE model of an underwater robotic arm in JAX on top of differentiable MuJoCo simulation. Before that, as an undergraduate in Dr. Samuel Grauer’s lab at Penn State, I developed Neural Optical Flow, a neural method for measuring fluid flows from images, which became my honors thesis and a paper in Experiments in Fluids. I am looking for roles in robot learning, especially work on vision-language-action models and world action models.

I also enjoy engineering projects across manufacturing, computer architecture, and machine learning, and I like building things end to end, from CAD and circuit boards to firmware and software. Right now, I am building a low-cost metal 3D printer and Bashputer, a 64-bit virtual machine written entirely in Bash.

I grew up in Chambersburg and Shippensburg, Pennsylvania, where I went to the Career Magnet School. I earned my bachelor’s degree in Mechanical Engineering from the Pennsylvania State University’s Schreyer Honors College.
